Copyright and Takedowns

Last updated: October 31, 2026

We respect the rights of the creators and owners of the podcast. If you own content that appears in JRE Clipper (whatwouldjoerogansay.com) and want it removed, tell us and we will act on it promptly. This policy follows the notice-and-takedown process of the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA).

What the Service contains

  • Episodes play in YouTube's embedded player from the official channel. To remove a video from YouTube itself, contact YouTube.
  • We show short transcript excerpts and timestamps as search results.
  • Signed-in users can make compilation videos of moments they select. These are stored by us.

We can remove any of these, and we can exclude a whole episode from search results and compilations.

How to send a takedown notice

Email vohoanvu96@gmail.com with the subject "Copyright notice", and include:

  1. the copyrighted work you believe is infringed (for example, the episode);
  2. where it appears in the Service: a link to the search results or compilation, or the episode and timestamp;
  3. your name, postal address, phone number and email address;
  4. a statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law;
  5. a statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the owner or are authorized to act on the owner's behalf;
  6. your physical or electronic signature (typing your full name is enough).

We aim to respond within 2 business days. When we remove content, we will tell the affected user if we can identify one.

Counter-notice

If your compilation was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, you can send a counter-notice to the same address with:

  1. identification of what was removed and where it appeared;
  2. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief it was removed by mistake or misidentification;
  3. your name, address and phone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the courts of your address (or, if you are outside the United States, any judicial district in which the Service may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the notice;
  4. your physical or electronic signature.

We will forward the counter-notice to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within 10 to 14 business days that they have filed a court action, we may restore the content.

Repeat infringers

We close the accounts of users who repeatedly infringe copyright.
